Making Tax Digital for Income Tax from April 2027

A short guide to who needs to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax from 6 April 2027, how the £30,000 test works, and when the quarterly updates are due.

Who needs to use it

GOV.UK, last updated 26 March 2026, says you need to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax if all of the following apply:

  • you are a sole trader or a landlord registered for Self Assessment
  • you get income from self-employment or property, or both
  • your qualifying income is more than the relevant threshold for the tax year

When you need to start depends on your qualifying income in a given tax year:

Qualifying income more thanMeasured on tax yearYou need to use it from
£50,0002024 to 20256 April 2026
£30,0002025 to 20266 April 2027
£20,0002026 to 20276 April 2028

If your qualifying income was over £50,000 in 2024 to 2025, GOV.UK says you should already have started from 6 April 2026, and you can still sign up if you have not. Partnerships will need to use it in the future; GOV.UK says it will set out that timeline later.

HMRC may write to you if your income is above the threshold, but GOV.UK says that if you did not receive a letter it is still your responsibility to check and to sign up in time. There is a GOV.UK tool to check if you need to use it, when you need to start, and whether you may be exempt.

What counts as qualifying income

GOV.UK’s qualifying-income page, last updated 11 September 2026, defines qualifying income as your total income from self-employment and property. That is the amount before expenses (also known as turnover), based on the tax return you submitted in the previous tax year. It can come from more than one self-employment or property source. In HMRC’s example, £25,000 of rental income plus £27,000 of self-employment income is £52,000 of qualifying income.

These other sources do not count towards qualifying income: employment (PAYE), your share of profit from a partnership as an individual partner, dividends (including those from your own company), a State Pension, and private pensions.

If all of your self-employment and property income had ceased before 6 April 2026, GOV.UK says you will not need to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax. Some people are exempt, including if they are digitally excluded, and there is an automatic exemption if qualifying income is £20,000 or less. Read GOV.UK’s exemption guidance, last updated 12 November 2026, rather than this page, for the full list and how to apply.

Quarterly update dates

Once you are in, you keep digital records and send quarterly updates through compatible software. GOV.UK says a quarterly update is a summary of self-employment and property income and expenses, not a tax return. You do not need to make accounting or tax adjustments before you send it, and HMRC does not receive individual receipts or invoices in the update. If you had no income or expenses in a period, you must still send the update.

There are two types of update period. The deadlines are the same for both. Standard periods, which align to the tax year, are the default:

Standard update periodDeadline
6 April to 5 July7 August
6 April to 5 October7 November
6 April to 5 January7 February
6 April to 5 April7 May (the following tax year)

So if you start on 6 April 2027, the standard deadlines for 2027 to 2028 are 7 August 2027, 7 November 2027, 7 February 2028, and 7 May 2028. Calendar periods (for an accounting period of 1 April to 31 March) use the same four deadline dates, with different period end dates. HMRC’s 2 November 2026 news item also states that a quarterly update is not a tax return and that no payment is due when you send it.

Software, and what a spreadsheet cannot do

Updates must be sent through compatible software. A spreadsheet or this website cannot do that. If you keep records in a spreadsheet, you need HMRC-recognised bridging software with a digital link. Use HMRC’s software finder. HMRC does not recommend a particular product. The £19 Set Aside UK kit is a planning workbook (tax pot, VAT-threshold watch, invoices and chasers). It is still an estimate, not tax advice, and still not Making Tax Digital software.

Penalties, in brief

GOV.UK’s penalties page, last updated 30 March 2026, says there are no penalties for missing a quarterly update deadline in the 2026 to 2027 tax year, though you still need to keep digital records and send the updates before you can submit your tax return. For tax years after 2026 to 2027, a late quarterly update or a late tax return can give you a penalty point. The threshold is 4 points, and then a £200 penalty, plus £200 each time you miss another submission deadline.

That is a short summary of the published page. Check the live penalties guidance before you rely on it, because later announcements may not yet appear there.

Questions

Do I need to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax from April 2027?

If you are a sole trader or landlord registered for Self Assessment, you have self-employment or property income, and your qualifying income for 2025 to 2026 was over £30,000, GOV.UK says you will need to use it from 6 April 2027. Use HMRC’s checker for your own figures. This is not tax advice.

What is qualifying income for Making Tax Digital?

Total income from self-employment and property, before expenses, based on the tax return you submitted in the previous tax year. PAYE, dividends, pensions, and a partner’s share of partnership profit do not count.

When are the quarterly updates due if I start in April 2027?

The standard deadlines are 7 August 2027, 7 November 2027, 7 February 2028, and 7 May 2028. The update is a summary, not a tax return, and it goes through compatible software.

Can I use a spreadsheet instead of Making Tax Digital software?

Not on its own. You need compatible software, or bridging software if your records are in a spreadsheet. This page and the kit cannot send updates to HMRC.

No. It is a summary of published GOV.UK guidance, checked on 17 November 2026 (Europe/London). Check the live pages or a qualified adviser before you act.
