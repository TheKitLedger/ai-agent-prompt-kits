Trading allowance: £1,000 now, £3,000 from 6 April 2027

A short guide to the UK trading allowance in 2026/27, and to the increase published on 28 October 2026, which is due to take effect from 6 April 2027.

What applies in 2026/27

The live GOV.UK page “Tax-free allowances on property and trading income”, when checked on 17 November 2026, still said you can get up to £1,000 each tax year in tax-free allowances for property or trading income. If you have both types of income, you get a £1,000 allowance for each. That page had last been updated on 8 May 2019.

On that page:

  • If your annual gross trading income is £1,000 or less, you may not have to tell HMRC, though there are circumstances when you must still register for Self Assessment and declare the income.
  • If your annual gross trading or property income is more than £1,000, you can use the tax-free allowance instead of deducting expenses or other allowances. This is called partial relief. You can deduct up to £1,000, but not more than the amount of your income.
  • If you use the allowances, you cannot also deduct other expenses or allowances. If your expenses are more than your income, GOV.UK says it may be beneficial to claim expenses instead of the allowances.
  • Gross income means the total amount you would put on your tax return before any allowances or expenses are taken off.
  • The trading allowance does not apply to trading income from a partnership.
  • You cannot use the allowances in a tax year if you have trade or property income from a company you or someone connected to you owns or controls, from a partnership where you or someone connected to you are partners, or from your employer or the employer of your spouse or civil partner.

If your gross income for a tax year is more than £1,000, the same page says you must register for Self Assessment by 5 October in the following tax year. The current Self Assessment registration page, checked on the same day, is written for this cycle: you must tell HMRC by 5 October 2026 if you need to complete a tax return for 2025 to 2026 and you have not sent a return before, or you registered before but did not need to send a return for 2024 to 2025. Use the live page for the date that applies to you.

What is due to change from 6 April 2027

A GOV.UK policy paper, “Increasing the trading allowance”, published on 28 October 2026, says:

  • The government is increasing the trading allowance from £1,000 to £3,000.
  • The change will have effect from 6 April 2027, for the 2027 to 2028 tax year onwards.
  • The Income Tax Self Assessment reporting threshold for trading income will also become £3,000 from that date.
  • Legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27 will change the current law in Chapter 1, Part 6A of the Income Tax (Trading and Other Income) Act 2005.

The paper’s impact table estimates that up to 1.4 million individuals could benefit, either from a lower tax bill or from simpler administration. Of those, 400,000 are estimated to have a reduction in their tax bill of £380 on average, and 100,000 are estimated to no longer need to report their trading income to HMRC in line with the increased reporting threshold. Those are government estimates from the policy paper, not a forecast of how many people will buy a kit, and not a calculation for any named person.

Until the main allowances page is updated, treat 2026/27 as the £1,000 year, and treat the £3,000 figure as a published policy that is due to take effect on 6 April 2027. Check both pages again if you are reading this after that date.

Questions

Is the UK trading allowance still £1,000?

Yes, for 2026/27, according to the live GOV.UK allowances page checked on 17 November 2026. That page had last been updated on 8 May 2019.

When does the trading allowance rise to £3,000?

The 28 October 2026 policy paper says from 6 April 2027, for 2027 to 2028 onwards, with the reporting threshold also becoming £3,000. Legislation is described as going into Finance Bill 2026-27. Check the live pages, because the main allowances page had not yet been rewritten when this guide was checked.

If I use the trading allowance, can I also claim expenses?

The allowances page says you cannot deduct other expenses or allowances if you claim the allowances. If your expenses are more than your income, it may be better to claim expenses instead. This is not tax advice.

Do I need to register for Self Assessment if I have a small side hustle?

If your annual gross trading income is more than £1,000, the allowances page says you must register by 5 October in the following tax year. There are also cases where you must register even if income is £1,000 or less. Use the live GOV.UK pages for your year.

No. It is a summary of published GOV.UK pages, checked on 17 November 2026 (Europe/London).
