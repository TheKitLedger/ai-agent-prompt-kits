VAT registration threshold explained (£90,000 rolling 12 months)

The VAT test is taxable turnover over any 12 months. It is not your profit, and it is not the 2026/27 tax year used by the Income Tax calculator.

The two tests

GOV.UK says you must register for VAT if either:

  • your total taxable turnover for the last 12 months goes over £90,000, or
  • you expect your taxable turnover to go over £90,000 in the next 30 days.

You can choose to register if turnover is under £90,000. That is voluntary registration. You do not have to register if you only sell goods and services that are VAT exempt or out of scope.

Taxable turnover is the value of what you sell that is not exempt or out of scope. It includes zero-rated sales as well as reduced-rated and standard-rated sales. GOV.UK also counts some items that are easy to miss, including goods you bartered or gave as gifts, and certain reverse-charge services. Profit after expenses is a different number. A quiet profit can still sit on turnover that has gone over £90,000.

When you have to register

If the last 12 months went over £90,000, you have to register within 30 days of the end of the month in which you went over. The effective date of registration is the first day of the second month after you went over.

HMRC’s example: on 15 July, taxable turnover for the previous 12 months is £100,000 for the first time. You must register by 30 August. The effective date of registration is 1 September.

If you realise turnover will go over £90,000 in the next 30 days, you have to register by the end of that 30-day period. The effective date is the date you realised, not the date the turnover actually crosses the line.

HMRC’s example: on 1 May you arrange a £100,000 contract and you will be paid at the end of May. You must apply by 30 May. The effective date is 1 May.

If you register late, VAT is due on sales since the date you should have registered. You might also have to pay a penalty. GOV.UK says that depends on how much you owe and how late the registration is.

You can apply for an exception if turnover goes over the threshold only temporarily. If most of your taxable supplies are zero-rated, you can ask HMRC for an exemption from registration. A non-established taxable person needs all of their taxable supplies to be zero-rated to be eligible. HMRC writes to say whether the request is granted.

Questions

What is the UK VAT registration threshold?

Register if taxable turnover for the last 12 months goes over £90,000, or if you expect it to go over £90,000 in the next 30 days. Registration under £90,000 is voluntary.

Is the £90,000 VAT test the same as the tax year or profit?

Neither. It is any rolling 12 months of taxable turnover. Zero-rated sales count. Exempt and out-of-scope sales do not. Profit is what the tax pot calculator uses for Income Tax and Class 4.
