Payments on account explained (first-year Self Assessment)

Why a first Self Assessment year often has nothing to pay in January 2027, and why 31 January 2028 can be the 2026/27 bill plus a first instalment towards the next year.

What they are

Payments on account are payments towards your next tax bill, including Class 4 National Insurance if you are self-employed. They are two instalments. Each is half of the tax you owed last year. They are due by midnight on 31 January and 31 July.

You must make both unless either:

  • the tax you owed last year was less than £1,000, or
  • last year you paid more than 80% of the tax you owed outside Self Assessment, for example through your tax code.

If you expect this year to be lower, you can ask HMRC to reduce the instalments, online or with form SA303. If you reduce them too far, interest is charged on the difference.

HMRC’s £3,000 example

GOV.UK publishes this example for someone who did not make payments on account last year, which includes a first time filing Self Assessment. The bill is £3,000.

  • The total due on 31 January is £4,500. That is the £3,000 bill plus a first instalment of £1,500 towards the next year.
  • The second instalment of £1,500 is due on 31 July.

HMRC’s write-up uses a 2023 to 2024 bill, due on 31 January 2025, with the July instalment on 31 July 2025. The arithmetic is the rule. It is not a 2026/27 rate.

Applying that rule to a first 2026/27 year

The 2026/27 tax year runs from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. HMRC’s deadline page says the tax year that ended on 5 April 2026 is paid by 11:59pm on 31 January 2027, with a second deadline of 31 July for payments on account. The same following-January pattern puts the 2026/27 bill on 31 January 2028. HMRC’s Making Tax Digital timeline lists 31 January 2028 as the date to submit the 2026 to 2027 return.

If 2026/27 is your first year, you usually pay on 31 January 2028, not in January 2027:

  • the full 2026/27 bill, plus
  • the first payment on account towards 2027/28, if the bill is £1,000 or more and you have not paid more than 80% of it outside Self Assessment.

The second instalment is then due on 31 July 2028. If the eventual bill for the next year is higher than the two instalments, HMRC’s guide says you also make a balancing payment the following 31 January, together with the first instalment towards the year after that.

On a £30,000 profit, with no other income and the England, Wales and Northern Ireland rates used by the calculator, the estimated 2026/27 bill is £4,531.80. Half of that is £2,265.90. The first-year 31 January 2028 figure is £6,797.70, and 31 July 2028 is £2,265.90. That £4,531.80 is the same total as the set-aside guide. It is an estimate, not a bill from HMRC.

If you already had a 2025/26 bill

The first year is the case this page is about. If you did owe tax for 2025/26, 31 January 2027 can include a balancing payment for that year plus a first instalment towards 2026/27. Questions

When is the first large Self Assessment payment if 2026/27 is my first year?

Usually by midnight on 31 January 2028: the 2026/27 bill plus the first instalment towards 2027/28. The second instalment is due on 31 July 2028. January 2027 is usually not that bill.

What is HMRC’s published payments on account example?

A £3,000 bill and no earlier payments on account. 31 January is £4,500 (£3,000 plus £1,500). 31 July is £1,500.

When are payments on account not required?

If last year’s tax was less than £1,000, or if you paid more than 80% of that tax outside Self Assessment. You can ask HMRC to reduce them. Interest can be charged if the reduction is too large.
