How payments on account work

Payments on account are two instalments towards your next Self Assessment bill, including Class 4 National Insurance if you are self-employed. Each payment is usually half of the tax you owed last year. They are due by midnight on 31 January and 31 July.

You must make them unless the tax you owed last year was less than £1,000, or last year you paid more than 80% of the tax you owed outside Self Assessment, for example through PAYE. You can ask HMRC to reduce them if you expect this year to be lower; interest can be charged if you reduce them too far.

If 2026/27 is your first year of Self Assessment, you usually pay the full 2026/27 bill on 31 January 2028, plus the first instalment towards 2027/28 — not in January 2027. HMRC’s published example uses a £3,000 bill: January becomes £4,500 (£3,000 plus a first instalment of £1,500), and July is £1,500.

Source: GOV.UK, Payments on account. When checked on 14 November 2026, HMRC’s Self Assessment estimator was labelled for 2025 to 2026 and said it would not take payments towards your next tax bill into account.

What this calculator leaves out

  • Scottish Income Tax, which uses different bands and rates
  • Student loan repayments
  • The High Income Child Benefit Charge
  • Marriage Allowance
  • Savings and dividend income, and capital gains
  • VAT you may have to charge or pay

It treats your trading profit as your only income when applying the Personal Allowance taper above £100,000. Class 2 National Insurance is explained in the result, but voluntary Class 2 is not added to the pot automatically.
